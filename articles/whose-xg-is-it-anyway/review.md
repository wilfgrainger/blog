# Editorial and visual review

Reviewed 23 September 2026. Scope: “Whose xG Is It Anyway?”, its four final images and its companion example.

The original had a strong fictional player and a useful distinction between successful tool calls and a valid decision, but reached its story late and repeated the architectural argument. The initial image refresh improved legibility without making enough of the article's particular argument. This revision gives the story, figures and runnable example the same evidence to work with.

## Final changes

- Open at 10:06 with the failed shortlist; keep Mark's two questions, football humour and the return to human scouting judgement. Reduce the article to roughly 2,800 words.
- Retain the original red-herring cover, then use three bespoke explanatory figures: a metric decomposition, a mandatory screening boundary, and an ownership timeline with an actual £9m-versus-£8m decision.
- Replace the broad architectural progression with specific responsibilities for definitions, mappings, evidence, validation and permission. A graph or ontology is an option with a job to do, not a mandatory maturity step.
- Separate the penalty error from provider compatibility. Separate a player who fails the brief from a record that cannot support a comparison. Ward leads only the validated subset.
- Add a runnable three-player CSV and dependency-free Python screen. It reproduces the flawed ranking, the corrected outcomes and a full refusal when all records use an unapproved model.
- Make the limits visible: fictional data, supplied age and budget facts, aggregate alignment assumed, provider truth not independently established, and no claim that these are LLM evaluation results.
- Distinguish legal effective time, publication time and decision time. Label the financial mandate as fictional and simplified.
- Retain primary references supporting the specific claims. Treat the Bank/FCA consortium minutes as member discussions rather than regulatory instructions.

## Validation

The companion command runs successfully. All nine behavior tests pass, covering the penalty-driven false positive, strict unrounded threshold, missing or invalid evidence, incompatible provenance, duplicate identities, distinct decision reasons, and full-cohort withholding. These checks verify the small supplied fixture and boundary, not a production recruitment system.

The SVGs have editable text, titles and extended alternatives. Their PNG exports have been inspected at article and phone widths. The xG bars share an axis and use unrounded rates. The financial figure holds amounts and currency constant while changing only the parent relationship. Alt text and captions state the interpretation in the article itself.

The revised article and example were cross-checked for numerical and policy consistency. The main primary references were checked against the claims retained in the text. This review does not independently reproduce the cited organisations' work.
